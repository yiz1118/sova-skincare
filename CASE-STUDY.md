# SOVA — Skincare E-Commerce Concept

**Concept Project** · Fictional independent skincare and wellness brand · 2026

## Concept

SOVA imagines daily skincare as a calm, understandable ritual. The collection is intentionally small: six fictional products across cleanser, serum, moisturizer, treatment, and body care. Its positioning, “Daily care, thoughtfully composed,” brings scientific clarity and a softer human tone together.

## Industry

Beauty, skincare, wellness, and direct-to-consumer e-commerce. SOVA is a portfolio exercise; it is not an operating brand. Products, formulations, packaging, prices, photography, and transactions are concepts.

## Brand Direction

The approved direction was **botanical science**. Warm cream, mineral green, deep olive, and restrained clay avoid familiar pink beauty cues. DM Sans keeps product information legible, while Instrument Serif gives selected headlines an editorial softness. Original AI-generated still lifes pair matte packaging, limestone, oat stems, serum drops, and cream textures. Product names and information live in accessible HTML rather than being embedded in images.

## Business Goals

The primary design goal is a clear path from discovery to product selection and a simulated bag. Secondary goals are ingredient understanding, trust through transparent concept labeling, and simple routine guidance. No conversion or revenue outcome is claimed.

## Shopping Experience

The home page moves from brand idea to concept assortment, product philosophy, ingredient education, routine guidance, body care, and a demo newsletter. The shop supports category filtering and sorting with URL state. Product cards show category, size, name, purpose, and price. The bag persists locally and allows quantity changes, removal, and a clearly labeled demonstration checkout. No payment is processed.

## Product Detail UX

Each product has its own page with image gallery, price, size, short description, cosmetic benefits, ingredient links, usage, suitable skin feel, routine placement, quantity control, and related products. A sticky mobile purchase bar keeps the next step visible. Copy avoids disease treatment claims and does not imply that these fictional finished formulas were tested.

## Routine UX

Three nonmedical questions ask how skin feels, how many steps the visitor prefers, and their cosmetic priority. A deterministic mapping recommends cleanser and moisturizer for an essentials routine, adds a serum for an extended routine, and optionally suggests an evening mask for a dry or tight feel. Results explain each step and its order, and visitors can edit answers, restart, or add the routine to the bag. The flow does not diagnose skin conditions.

## Mobile

The layout moves to a compact navigation, scrollable category chips, two-column catalog, stacked product detail, and sticky purchase action. Browser checks cover 360, 390, 430, 768, 1024, and 1440 px. The design includes visible focus states and reduced-motion support.

## Technology

Next.js App Router, React, strict TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, locally served fonts, structured product data, localStorage bag persistence, Playwright browser checks, and automated accessibility checks. Shopping behavior is client-side and needs no account, backend, or API credentials.

## What This Project Demonstrates

Original beauty brand art direction translated into a complete store; clear product data and selection tools; a restrained mobile commerce interface; explainable cosmetic routine guidance; honest content boundaries for a concept project; and a tested implementation path from product discovery to simulated checkout.
